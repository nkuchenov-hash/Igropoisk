Фактическая база

  • Steam's current official page describes Wolfenstein 3D as developed by id Software in 1992 and as a landmark World War II first-person shooter that helped start the modern FPS genre.
  • The player controls Allied agent William B.J. Blazkowicz. The story setup involves Operation Eisenfaust, capture in Castle Wolfenstein and an escape through hostile corridors while investigating the Nazi project.
  • The central interaction loop is extremely direct: move through maze-like levels, open doors, search rooms and secrets, collect health/ammunition/treasure, and shoot enemies from a first-person view.
  • The original technology creates a fast pseudo-3D first-person environment rather than a modern fully polygonal 3D world. Enemies and objects are sprite-based and the level geometry is strongly grid/corridor oriented.
  • PC Gamer's history of the game reports that speed became a major design priority. Earlier stealth-like ideas inherited from Castle Wolfenstein were removed when they slowed the action; the final game emphasized rapid movement, mazes, exploration, secrets and shooting.
  • GamesRadar retrospectives similarly describe Wolfenstein 3D as a major milestone that popularized the FPS form rather than literally inventing every first-person idea. Its importance lies in turning earlier ideas into a fast, accessible and highly influential action template.
  • The game predates Doom and directly precedes id Software's later refinement of the formula. Modern discussion therefore has to separate historical importance from how the mechanics feel today.
  • The original design has no modern vertical aiming structure, highly repetitive corridor geometry and a comparatively small mechanical vocabulary by later FPS standards.
  • Secrets, hidden rooms and treasure are an important part of the level rhythm and give exploration a second purpose beyond simply reaching exits.
  • The game's presentation is deliberately pulpy and exaggerated rather than a sober historical simulation.
